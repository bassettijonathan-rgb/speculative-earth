How this world works

  • The planet is a grid of 10,242 cells about 240 km apart. A dozen rigid plates drift on fixed rotation poles, collide to raise mountains, subduct, rift apart and weld together.
  • Climate comes from latitude, height, distance from the sea, prevailing winds and CO₂. It is recalculated every million years, not seasonally.
  • Life starts from 36 real survivors of today, from rats and crows to grasses and krill. Each species has a range, a body size, a diet and a temperature it likes. Species split when their range is cut in two or when part of the population takes up a new way of life, and die out when the land under them changes faster than they can follow.
  • Every world is one million-year tick at a time, from one random seed, so your step size never changes the outcome.
